Why this site exists

Most explanations of technical things are written by experts, for other experts. I’d rather build things up from scratch, the way I wish they’d been explained to me, and make them make sense to anyone who’s curious.

There’s also a thread running through all of it: AI is only as big as the electricity behind it. Every data centre needs enormous amounts of power, and getting a new one connected to the grid can take years. So to understand where AI is heading, you have to understand electricity. That’s why the very first chapter here is about what electricity actually is.

What I’m learning right now: the bottlenecks

The long-term picture that excites me is a post-scarcity economy: a world where AI and robots make most goods and services so cheap to produce that they’re effectively abundant. We’re not there yet, and the interesting question is what stands in the way. So that’s what I’m learning: the bottlenecks.

02 · Intelligence

AI that keeps learning

Today’s AI mostly stops learning once it’s trained. Getting it to keep learning, the way people do, is one of the big unsolved problems.

03 · Physical AI

Training data for robots

Robots need huge amounts of real-world experience to learn from, which is far harder to collect than text. I’m building a robot vacuum on a Raspberry Pi 5 to learn this hands-on.

04 · What stays scarce

Energy, grid connections, land

Even in a world of abundance, some things stay limited: the energy itself, connections to it, and land, especially in the places people want to be.

And underneath it all, I’m learning electronics from the ground up, from logic gates to how a computer’s memory works.

How I write

  • From scratch. No assumed knowledge; every idea is built from the one before it.
  • Every number explained. If a figure or formula appears, there’s a note on where it comes from.
  • Sources cited. News pieces link to where every figure came from.
  • Explanation, not advice. I explain what’s happening in markets; I never tell anyone what to buy or sell.
  • Corrections welcome. If I’ve got something wrong, tell me and I’ll fix it.
